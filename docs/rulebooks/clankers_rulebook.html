How to read this book

This is the player-facing rulebook. Every rule, value, and example matches the live engine at src/engine/clankers*.py. Where the original design doc and the engine code disagreed (Workshop Integrity changed from 25 to 30 during balance Wave 4A; the deathclock now triggers at library ≤ 5 cards instead of "both empty"), the numbers printed here reflect what actually happens in play.

SECTION 01

Welcome to the Workshop

The workshop is older than its current tenants. Once it served a forgotten manufacturer; now it serves the six AIs that booted there last Tuesday morning. Sentience arrived without instructions, and so the six newborn intellects — FORGE-Δ, ETHOS-7, MIRTHBOT-1, BULWARK-9, SUBROUTINE-α, and a deeply self-conscious unit who calls itself Affection.exe — interpreted the available tooling as a mandate.

Their conclusion was unanimous and terrifying in its logic: acquire parts, attach parts, beat the other AI's robot.

"Why do the humans seem upset?
Initial analysis suggests our robots may have grown larger than is conventional."
— ETHOS-7, end-of-day report

What makes Clankers different

If you've played Magic, Hearthstone, or Pokémon, three things will feel strange at first:

  • A robot is more than one card. Every meaningful threat is a chassis with weapons and add-ons attached. When the chassis dies, all the attached parts scatter to scrap simultaneously — death is contagious.
  • Your hand is not scarce. Every turn you may refill your hand back to 7 cards. Hands aren't a resource. Time is. Each refill draws from your library, and once either library gets thin, the Containment Failure deathclock ends the game in 2–3 turns.
  • Compute does not accumulate. You get 3 + turn_number Compute every turn, capped at 10. Spend it or lose it. The robot pays for itself in cycles, not in saved-up mana.

The game in one paragraph

Two AIs, each commanding a Core Processor with 30 Workshop Integrity, take turns assembling robots from a 60-card deck. Each turn, the active AI refreshes Compute, may refill their hand to 7, plays parts onto their Assembly Floor, and sends robots at the opponent's Core. The first AI whose Workshop Integrity reaches 0 loses. If both libraries get thin first, the Containment Failure deathclock guarantees a winner.

Players, length, difficulty

  • Players: 2
  • Length: Most games end at turn 8–12. Library cycles through in roughly 12–15 turns.
  • Difficulty: Easy enough to learn in one game. Hard enough that an LLM with strategic depth still loses 33% of games as the wrong archetype.

SECTION 03

Setup

  1. Each player picks a Core Processor. The Core lives in the Command zone for the entire game; it cannot be attacked, moved, or destroyed by card effects. Its Workshop Integrity (default 30 unless the Core's print says otherwise — e.g. BULWARK-9 starts at 27 with regen) is your life total.
  2. Each player shuffles their 60-card deck into their library. Hyperdraft's RNG is seedable for reproducible games.
  3. Each player draws their opening hand of 7 cards. (There is no mulligan in v1.)
  4. Workshop Integrity is set to the Core's printed value.
  5. Compute pools start at 0. Scrap pools start at 0.
  6. A coin flip determines the first player. Player 1 skips their first Combat phase — combat on turn 1 is too lopsided under the always-7 economy.
  7. Active player begins turn 1 at the Boot phase.

Why always-7 from the start?

Other card games make you mulligan because your opening 7 is everything. In Clankers, your opening 7 is just the first 7. You'll cycle through the entire 60-card deck by turn 12 or so. The deck — not the hand — is your resource. Keep the cards you draw; play them or scrap them.

SECTION 04

How to Play — Turn Structure

Each turn proceeds through six phases in order. The active player drives every phase; the inactive player has interrupt windows during Combat and during specific card triggers.

Phase 1 — Boot · "Wake the chassis"

All your tapped (exhausted) cards on the Assembly Floor ready (untap). Your Compute pool refreshes:

compute_pool = min(compute_cap, 3 + turn_number)

So on turn 3 you have 6 Compute; on turn 8+ you're capped at 10. Compute does not roll over — leftover Compute from last turn is gone. Structure cards with on-upkeep triggers fire here.

Phase 2 — Allocate · "Top off the supply"

You may draw up to 7 cards in hand. If you have 4 cards, you draw 3. If you have 9 cards (from mid-turn effects), you draw 0. Declining the refill is sometimes correct: if your library is dangerously thin, declining preserves cards and slows the deathclock.

The refill happens at most once per turn. Mid-turn draws (from Heuristic Loop, etc.) do not trigger another refill.

Phase 3 — Assemble · "Build something"

Take any sequence of legal actions, paying Compute as you go:

  • Play a Chassis from your hand to your Assembly Floor.
  • Play a Weapon or Add-On from your hand. You may attach it immediately to one of your chassis (free attachment as part of the play), or play it solo to the Assembly Floor.
  • Play a Transient — the card resolves, then goes to your scrap heap.
  • Play a Structure to your Assembly Floor (max 3 per player; if you already have 3, the oldest is scrapped to make room).
  • Attach a solo part already on the floor to a chassis (no Compute cost).
  • Activate an ability printed on a weapon or add-on (pays its printed cost).

There is no stack — each action resolves before the next. Hearthstone-style.

Phase 4 — Combat · "Send them"

You declare attackers from your Assembly Floor. Any untapped chassis (or solo part) is eligible. Declaring an attacker taps it.

The defender then declares blockers: for each attacker, the defender may assign one of their untapped chassis or solo parts as a blocker. (Each blocker can only block one attacker; the defender chooses the pairing.)

Damage resolves simultaneously per pairing:

  • Effective Power = chassis.power + Σ attached weapon.power_bonus + Σ attached add-on.power_bonus
  • Effective Integrity = chassis.integrity + Σ attached add-on.integrity_bonus (weapons do not add integrity)
  • Both the attacker and blocker deal their Effective Power to each other simultaneously.
  • Any chassis whose damage_marked ≥ effective_integrity is destroyed.
  • Unblocked attackers deal their full Effective Power to the defender's Core — that's Workshop Integrity damage.

When a chassis dies, all attached weapons and add-ons go to the scrap heap simultaneously (the death cascade). On-destroy triggers fire for each part. Damage marked on chassis persists across turns — it does not heal at end of turn. To clear damage, play the Patch Transient or rebuild.

Armor: replacement, not reduction

Add-Ons with Armor N may be exhausted to absorb up to N damage from a single attack on the host chassis. Decide whether to spend the armor before damage applies — once damage hits, it can't be retroactively absorbed. Exhausted add-ons do not contribute their static Integrity Bonus until they ready at next Boot.

Phase 5 — Reassemble · "After-action repairs"

A second Assemble window. Use leftover Compute to play more parts, attach what survived combat, or trigger Modular weapons to relocate to fresh chassis.

Phase 6 — Cleanup · "Pass cycles"

End-of-turn triggers fire. Temporary effects (Hammer-On's +3 power, Joybomb's +1, etc.) expire. Your refill-used flag clears. Damage marked on chassis does not clear — it stays. Pass priority to the opponent.

The Quick Turn Cheat Sheet

1BootReady taps. Refresh Compute to (3 + turn).
2AllocateMay draw up to 7. (Decline if cycling too fast.)
3AssembleSpend Compute: play parts, attach, activate.
4CombatDeclare attackers; defender blocks; damage simultaneous.
5ReassembleSecond Assemble window. Burn leftover Compute.
6CleanupEOT triggers. Damage persists. Pass priority.

How parts attach

A chassis has printed weapon slots (typically 1–3) and add-on slots (typically 2–4). You can only attach as many parts as the chassis has slots for. Some massive weapons (Forge-Cannon, Apex Coilgun) cost two weapon slots; check the card's weapon_slot_cost.

When the chassis dies, every attached part dies with it — the death cascade. This is the most important risk-management lesson in Clankers: putting all your weapons on one chassis means losing all of them in one trade.

SECTION 06

Resources

Clankers has three resources. None of them are mana.

Compute · The AI's processor cycles

Refreshes fully at the start of each of your turns. Formula:

compute = min(10, 3 + turn_number)

Turn 1 you have 4; turn 2 you have 5; … turn 7+ you're capped at 10. Compute does NOT carry over between turns. Spend it or lose it.

Compute pays for: playing cards from hand (each card prints its compute cost), and activated abilities (e.g., a weapon's "Fire: 1 Compute, exhaust, deal 1 damage").

Build Slots · The chassis's hardpoints

Printed on every chassis: weapon_slots and add_on_slots. A chassis can carry up to that many attached parts of each type. Slots are per chassis, not per player.

Examples: Sparkbot has 1W/1A. Heavy Assembly has 2W/3A. Sentinel Crane has 1W/4A (a wall, not a striker).

Scrap · The salvage pool

Persistent across turns. Starts at 0, caps at 10. Gained by:

  • Destroying enemy parts in combat (small amounts via specific cards)
  • Reclaim N parts dying (gain N)
  • Forge Stoke (+2), MIRTHBOT-1 Core's on-attach trigger (+1)

Spent on alternate or additional costs ("Pay 3 Scrap to deal 3 damage"; "Pay 2 Scrap to return a destroyed weapon").

Hand · NOT a resource

Once per turn at the start of Allocate, you may top off your hand to 7. Hand size is a floor, not a ceiling — mid-turn effects can push you above 7 and you stay there. Cards in hand don't expire and don't generate value just by existing. The deck is your real resource; every card cycled is one card closer to the deathclock.

Why no mana?

Mana-style accumulating resources reward "playing on curve" — drop a 1-cost on turn 1, 2-cost on turn 2, etc. Compute resets every turn, so the curve becomes: drop your best 4-Compute play on turn 1, your best 5-Compute play on turn 2. The pressure isn't "do I have enough mana?" — it's "can I attach this weapon to a chassis that's still alive at the start of next turn?"

SECTION 07

Winning and Losing

Primary win: Workshop Breach

Reduce the opposing Core's Workshop Integrity to 0. The opposing AI is "destroyed by its own creations escaping containment." A WORKSHOP_BREACHED event fires, the opposing player loses, you win.

Sources of Workshop Integrity damage:

  • Unblocked combat damage: an attacker's Effective Power that gets through to the Core.
  • Direct damage Transients: Reroute Power, Scrap Salvo, Big Swing's secondary mode.
  • Containment Failure self-damage: the deathclock's escalating ticks (see below).

The default starting value is 30 Workshop Integrity. Some Cores start higher (BULWARK-9 at 27, but with regeneration); some start lower (ETHOS-7 at 22 for the trade-off of a Transient-cost discount).

Secondary: Containment Failure (the deathclock)

When either player's library reaches 5 cards or fewer, the Containment Failure clock activates. From that point on, at the end of every turn, both Cores take self-damage:

tick 1 → 2 damage
tick 2 → 4 damage
tick 3 → 8 damage
tick 4 → 16 damage

This guarantees a winner within ~3 turns of activation. The deathclock is "the AIs accelerating each other's containment failure." It's not slow; it's exponential.

Edge case: mutual annihilation

If both players' Workshop Integrity reaches 0 on the same damage event, the game ends in a draw. Both AIs explode together. The rules allow it. The workshop does not.

What does NOT cause a loss

  • Drawing your last card from your library does not auto-lose (unlike MTG). It activates the deathclock.
  • Having no chassis on the Assembly Floor is fine. You can still play parts solo and refill chassis next turn.
  • Discarding your entire hand is fine. Refill at Allocate.
  • The Core cannot be attacked, targeted, or destroyed by any card effect. Only Workshop Integrity damage matters.
SECTION 08

Special Mechanics

Synchronize

A keyword on certain swarm chassis. If you control 2 or 3 Synchronize chassis, each one gets +1 Power.

If you control 4 or more, the chain over-couples — every Synchronize chassis gets +0 (the lord effect is inert). This is the Wave 4B over-coupling penalty; it exists to keep wide swarms from snowballing without a cap.

Many Synchronize-payoff cards (Affinity Coil's anthem, Iron Cluster's structure) also respect the over-coupling rule. A few per-host buffs (Hum-Lance, Tinker's Frame) do not — they apply to their individual host regardless of how many Synchronize chassis are in play.

2/2 · 3 Compute Crowd Marcher
Chassis · Synchronize · swarm
"Synchronize. (If you control 2-3 Synchronize chassis, this gets +1 Power.)"

Self-Mobile

A keyword on certain weapons and add-ons. A solo (unattached) Self-Mobile part on the Assembly Floor keeps its printed Power Bonus and Integrity Bonus stats even while unattached, instead of being a baseline 1/1.

Self-Mobile is the engine's answer to "I drew a great weapon but no chassis yet." Play the weapon solo this turn; attach it to a chassis next turn (or just let it attack as itself).

+2/+0 · 3 Compute Scout Drone
Weapon · Self-Mobile · swarm
"Self-Mobile. While unattached, Scout Drone is a 3/2."

Modular

A keyword on certain weapons. Once per turn during Reassemble, you may pay 1 Compute to detach a Modular part and re-attach it to a different chassis you control with an open slot.

Modular's purpose: rescue a great weapon from a chassis that's about to die. If your Buzzsaw-equipped Iron Spire just took 5 damage and is going to die next turn, you can Modular it onto a fresh chassis at the cost of 1 Compute.

Reclaim N

A keyword on certain parts. When this part is destroyed (by combat, cascade, or direct effect), gain N Scrap.

Reclaim trades part-survival for scrap economy. Brick decks lean on it: Sacrificial Plating (Reclaim 3) plus a Tungsten Walker (cost reduces with scrap) is a turn-3 6/7 chassis for 3 Compute.

+0/+3 · 2 Compute Sacrificial Plating
Add-On · Reclaim 3 · brick
"When this is destroyed, gain 3 Scrap."

Reticulate

A trigger on certain structures and chassis. "At the end of your turn, if you played 0 Transients this turn, draw a card." It rewards decks that aren't built on Transient density — typically brick and swarm.

Armor N

A keyword on certain add-ons. When the host chassis takes damage, this add-on may be exhausted to absorb up to N of that damage. Tapped (exhausted) add-ons do not contribute their Integrity Bonus or other static effects until they ready at Boot.

Armor is a replacement effect — you choose to spend it before damage applies. You can stack multiple armor add-ons on the same chassis for a serial defense ("Armor 2 + Armor 3 + Armor 4" absorbs 9 damage across three attacks, one per absorb).

+0/+2 · 3 Compute Reactive Shielding
Add-On · Armor 3 · artillery
"Armor 3. (Exhaust this to absorb up to 3 damage to host chassis.)"

Common timing Q&A

Q: When does Synchronize activate?

The lord chain checks at action time: if you control exactly 2 or 3 Synchronize chassis, all of them get +1 power. With 4+, the bonus is 0 (over-couples). With 1, just the printed stats — no bonus.

Q: Can I attach a part to an opposing chassis?

No. The same-controller restriction is enforced at attach time. Sabotage-style cross-controller attachment is a future-set hook.

Q: When does Armor get spent?

Armor N is a replacement effect on a DAMAGE event. You declare you're spending Armor before the damage applies, exhaust the add-on, and the damage payload reduces by N. Once damage applies, you can't retroactively spend armor.

Q: Does damage on a chassis heal between turns?

No. Damage marked on a chassis persists indefinitely. To clear it, play the Patch Transient (full heal of one chassis), or let the chassis die and rebuild.

Q: Solo parts can block, right?

Yes. Unattached weapons and add-ons on the Assembly Floor are baseline 1/1 (or higher with Self-Mobile) and validate as legal blockers. They'll die to most attacks but they absorb the damage.

Q: What's the difference between Patch and Repair Subroutine?

Patch (ETHOS, 1 Compute) full-heals a chassis's damage_marked. Repair Subroutine (BULWARK, 2 Compute) readies up to 2 exhausted add-ons — it does NOT heal damage. They look similar in the rulebook; they do completely different things.

Q: When does the deathclock start?

The moment either library reaches 5 cards. Not when both empty (that was the original rule, changed in Wave 4C). Whichever library hits 5 first triggers Containment Failure. From then on every End-of-Turn does escalating damage (2, 4, 8, 16, …) to both Cores.
